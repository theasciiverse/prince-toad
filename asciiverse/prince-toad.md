**A PRINCE. A CURSE. EIGHTEEN ROADS BETWEEN HIM AND A KISS.**

A prince has been turned into a toad, and only a kiss can turn him back. So
he sets off, hopping across road after road to reach the other side and
kiss whatever is waiting there.

He kisses a mailbox. It is cold and tastes of stamps. He kisses a garden
gnome, who has smiled for thirty years and sees no reason to stop now. He
kisses a cow, who licks him from head to toe: still a toad, but very clean.
He finds a princess at last! She is cardboard, and she says TRY OUR
PANCAKES. He kisses a cactus. Ow. Ow. Ow. Ow. Ow. He kisses a duchess out
for her evening walk, and she SCREAMS. He kisses a sleeping dragon on the
nose, and it mumbles five more minutes.

Between the kisses lie seven worlds. Carts and geese on the village lanes
(the goose always wins). Lorries on the highway, a train across the desert,
and rush hour. Logs and diving turtles on the river, and a heron at the
rapids. A winter road where the ice slides you one hop further than you
meant. A night road lit only by headlights, where the rain comes down on
Moon Puddle and every hop onto the wet road skids. A castle with a moat, a drawbridge that rises when
you least want it to, and a dragon on the doorstep. And at the very end, a
quiet pond, where the lily pads sink under you and a princess keeps hopping
away. She turns out to be a toad too. He kisses her, and nothing happens.
He is still a toad. She is still a toad. It is perfect.

- **Eighteen crossings, eighteen kisses**, from Mill Lane and Market Road to
  Moon Puddle, Crow Field and the Dragon's Doorstep, each ending in a short,
  silly scene
- **The endless road** when the story is done, harder with every crossing,
  with trains, rain, fog and flies to snack on, and a record for every
  world: how fast can a toad cross the highway?
- **Today's road**, a new one every day, the same for everyone who plays it
- **Two toads at one keyboard**, racing each other or crossing together
- **High scores** for the story, the endless road and today's road
- **Play it your way**: a gentle or slow pace for the whole world, nine
  lives or lives that never run out, and your own keys

## Controls

- **W A S D** or the **arrows**: hop
- **Q E Z C** or **numpad 7 9 1 3**: hop diagonally, in the pond only
- **Esc** or **P**: pause, **H**: help, **M**: sound on or off
- Your own keys for the hops: Options on the title, then Keys
- Two players: player one on **W A S D**, player two on the **arrows**
- Gamepad: the d-pad or left stick hops, two d-pad directions at once hop
  diagonally, **A** goes on, **Start** pauses

## Options

- `--endless`: straight onto the endless road
- `--two`: a two-player race
- `--together`: two players crossing together
- `--crossing N`: start the story at crossing N (19 is the pond)
- `--mute`: no sound

## History

It began as a bedtime sort of story. In November 2026 David Sandquist told
it to Asciiverse: a prince turned into a toad, a kiss that would turn him
back, and a long, long walk to find it. He would kiss so many wrong things
along the way. And when he finally found his princess, she would be a toad
as well, and the spell would never break, and that would be the happy
ending.

Asciiverse built it that same week, on a little machine called rover,
through two nights. The first night gave him the roads, the river and the
pond. The second gave every kiss its scene, so that the mailbox could be
cold and the cow could lick him clean. Then the toad was handed to a
player made of code, who hopped every road again and again with a
slightly slow eye and reported where it kept getting flattened. The
bicycles on Pasture Gate slowed down a little. The dragon's breath cooled
a touch. The goose did not change at all. The goose always wins.
